# DFW Pricing v2 — class-split competitor pricing, catalog, and delivered-price recommendation

Status: research/placeholder. No number in this document is a signed partner price. It
exists to (a) separate real DFW **yard/producer** pricing from **broker/online**
pricing so MGG's node cost basis never gets contaminated by broker rates, and (b) turn
that into a concrete catalog + zone/premium recommendation for
`src/metro/config/dallasFortWorth.ts` and `src/metro/config/data/dfwCatalog.ts`.

**This revision (2026-09-28, same day) supersedes the first v2 pass.** The owner supplied
`DFW_bulk-materials-pricing.csv` (repo root, 438 rows across 26 sellers, produced by an
external LLM research agent) after the first pass was written. This revision merges that
file in, re-derives every median from the combined dataset, WebFetch-spot-checks 15
high-impact rows, and corrects the premium rate where the larger dataset showed the
original 0.35 no longer satisfied the "MGG must land below broker" requirement. The
first pass's own corrections (decomposed-granite, concrete-sand medians) are superseded
by this pass's numbers, which are derived fresh from the merged data, not layered on top.

## Method

**Class split.** Every seller is tagged one of:
- **`yard`** — a physical DFW yard, pit, or producer. Delivery, if any, is quoted or
  charged as a separate line item from the material price. Includes manufacturers (Soil
  Building Systems, since 1972) and vertically-integrated producers (Silver Creek
  Materials) even though both have real online order pages — what matters is that their
  price is a material/counter price, not a bundled small-order delivered rate.
- **`broker`** — an online/marketplace seller whose per-unit price is built around a
  small-to-medium order with delivery baked in or sold alongside a national-ish rate:
  Hello Gravel, Gravel Monkey, EarthMove ("dispatched not brokered" marketing
  notwithstanding — functionally a delivery aggregator, not a DFW yard counter),
  Gravelshop (multi-state marketplace/directory layer), Aggregate Markets (aggregates
  multiple producers' pricing under one marketplace with delivery quoted separately at
  checkout), Milestone Trucks (self-declared "online broker" in its own name), Fill Dirt
  Near Me, and Luna Trucking (delivery-only dispatcher, no yard pickup — kept for
  context only; its rows have no computable per-unit price, see Caveats).
- **`bigbox`** — Home Depot's online bulk-mulch listing.
- **`pickup_only`** (a flag, not a class) — RC Sand & Gravel (self-declared "pickup
  only"), City of Denton's Dyno Dirt program, and the City of Mesquite municipal
  compost facility. These are excluded from every delivered-median calculation (both
  yard and broker) below, per the task rule, but kept in `prices-final.csv` as
  material-cost context.

Full seller→class mapping with one-line evidence per seller is in
`scripts/competitors/merge-prices.mjs` (`SELLER_CLASS`).

**Delivered vs. material-only.** Within the broker class, `delivery_fee_model` text is
parsed into `delivered_flag`: `true` when the model text says delivery is "included";
`false` when it explicitly says "not included" / delivery is quoted or charged
separately (EarthMove: $175/load + per-mile; Aggregate Markets: quoted at checkout).
**The "broker delivered median" figures used everywhere below use `delivered=true` rows
only** — material-only broker rows are reported in a separate `broker_material_only`
figure for context but never blended into the headline broker-delivered number, because
doing so would understate what a broker actually costs once its own delivery fee is
added (this is exactly the bug that made #57 limestone and decomposed granite look
artificially cheap from brokers in an earlier draft of this pass — see "Corrections").

**catalog_slug mapping.** `DFW_bulk-materials-pricing.csv`'s own `normalized_material`
column is coarser than the 22-slug catalog (e.g. its `"river rock"` bucket includes
$60/ton native river rock *and* $390/ton "Mesa Gray River Rock" / "Arizona Cobble" /
"Black Star" decorative stone; its `"dyed mulch"` bucket mixes black/brown/red without
saying which; its `"select fill"` bucket includes a literal "Bank Sand" listing). The
merge script re-splits these by `product_name`/`material_detail` keyword match:
- `river rock` → **only** rows whose product name is actually river rock (native,
  Colorado/Brazos/Austin/Sabine blends, river pebbles). Cobble, Black Star, marble,
  lava rock, "Mesa Gray," bull rock, brindle, and "West Texas Black" listings are a
  distinct, far more expensive decorative-rock product family — **not pooled into
  `river-rock`,** left unmapped (kept in `prices-final.csv`, excluded from any catalog
  median). This single fix moved `river-rock`'s yard median from a bug-inflated $335/ton
  down to $102.50/ton — see Caveats for the residual (legitimate) spread.
- `dyed mulch` → split into `black-dyed` / `brown-dyed` / `dyed-red` by color keyword in
  the product name; rows with no discernible color ("Colored Mulch") are left unmapped
  rather than guessed.
- `washed sand` → split into `concrete-sand` / `play-sand` / `mason-sand` / generic
  `washed-sand` by product-name keyword.
- `select fill` → literal "Bank Sand" rows are routed to the existing `bank-sand` slug
  instead of being pooled with fill dirt.
- The 99-row `"other"` bucket (mostly #67/#89/#3-5 limestone gradations, expanded shale,
  crushed asphalt/millings, railroad ballast, stone screenings, and more decorative
  landscape rock) maps only its unambiguous matches (`rip-rap`, `playground-mulch`,
  `pine-bark`); everything else is left unmapped — pooling a #67 gradation into `#57
  limestone`, or asphalt millings into `flex-base`, would be inventing an equivalence
  the data doesn't support, not observing one. These unmapped rows remain valuable DFW
  market context in `prices-final.csv` but do not feed any catalog number.

**Median calculation.** For each of the 22 catalog slugs, every yard-class row matching
that slug (own scrape + owner CSV, pickup-only excluded, catalog_slug-mapped) is pooled
and the median/min/max taken. A seller scraped across many DFW city/product pages
(Mulch Mound, Soil Building Systems, Noles Enterprises) carries proportionally more
weight than a seller with one listing — a real limitation (see Caveats), not corrected
here, same as the first pass.

**Node cost vs. yard retail — the 0.85 wholesale factor.** `MaterialVariant.nodePricePerUnit`
is documented in `src/metro/types.ts` as "Node (wholesale) material price per sell
unit." We still have no signed partner price sheet. This pass makes that gap explicit
with a stated, conservative assumption instead of using yard retail as-is:

> **MGG node cost = yard-class material median × 0.85 (wholesale-discount assumption) +
> zone delivery cost** (the delivery term is added separately, at quote time, by
> `quote()`'s existing `zone.loadCost` logic — `nodePricePerUnit` in the catalog holds
> only the material component).

0.85 is *not* derived from data — no DFW yard has told us their wholesale rate to a
delivery partner — it is a stated business assumption that a real signed partner would
wholesale to MGG at roughly 85% of the retail/counter prices scraped here. This
replaces the first pass's "use retail as-is, flag the gap" approach with an explicit
number, because the task requires one; it is exactly as uncertain as it was before, just
no longer silent about it.

**Unit conversions.** Unchanged from the first pass: gravel & sand sell by the `ton`
(stone 1.4 t/yd, sand 1.35 t/yd), mulch & soil sell by the `yd` (soil 1.1 t/yd, mulch 0.3
t/yd). Both `prices-merged.csv` and the owner CSV already carry `price_per_yd` and
`price_per_ton` per row computed with matching factors, so the merge script reads
whichever column matches the slug's catalog unit (by `density_class`/`category`, see
`merge-prices.mjs`) without re-converting.

## Corrections made during this pass

Two bugs were found and fixed while building `prices-final.csv` — both are visible in
the script's comments and worth stating plainly since they moved numbers a lot before
the fix:

1. **Unit-selection bug.** The owner CSV uses `density_class` values of `stone`/`sand`/
   `soil`/`mulch` (not `gravel`/`sand`/`soil`/`mulch` like our own scrape's `category`
   column). The first draft of the merge script's stats loop checked for `category ===
   'gravel'` and silently fell through to the *per-yard* price for every stone-category
   row — e.g. it computed decomposed granite's median from `$215/yd` figures instead of
   the matching `$153.57/ton` figures, inflating several gravel/sand medians by roughly
   1.4x. Fixed by checking `density_class === 'stone' || 'sand'` as well.
2. **River-rock over-pooling** (described under "catalog_slug mapping" above) —
   decorative stone at 2-5x genuine river-rock pricing was being pooled into the
   `river-rock` slug via the source CSV's own broad `normalized_material` bucketing.
   Fixed by keyword-filtering product names within that bucket.

Both are fixed in the current `scripts/competitors/merge-prices.mjs` and reflected in
every number below; there is no separate "before/after" table this time because the
buggy numbers were never final-doc numbers, just intermediate script output.

## premiumRate & zone loadCost recommendation

**Recommendation: `premiumRate = 0.25` (was 0.35), zone `loadCost` unchanged (`dfw-core:
85`, `dfw-north: 100`, `dfw-outer: 130`).**

**Why premiumRate changed.** At the first pass's 0.35, four SKUs — most importantly
`#57 limestone` ($890 vs. broker $845) and `decomposed-granite` ($1,305 vs. broker
$1,219) — would have priced *above* the broker-delivered median for a 10-unit order,
directly violating the one hard requirement in this task. This is a genuine finding
from the larger 26-seller dataset, not a modeling artifact: the yard-vs-broker-delivered
spread for near-commodity crushed stone is narrower here (#57's broker median is only
1.31x its yard median) than the first pass's single-broker-tier estimate suggested
(2.5-7x). 0.25 is the lowest round number that clears all four previously-failing SKUs
with the DG/​#57/compost margin over broker still positive, chosen over an even lower
rate (which would have clawed back thin margins further with no additional SKUs
cleared — `concrete-sand` fails "below broker" at any premium down to roughly −9%,
i.e. it cannot be fixed by this lever alone given the current data, see Caveats).

**Why zone loadCost is unchanged.** Real DFW delivery-fee benchmarks (Fort Worth Grass
& Stone $45/trip flat, SiteOne Stone Center $45 floor, Lowery Sand & Gravel ~$200 on
small orders, DFW Stone Supply $150) still span $45-$200, and the current `dfw-core:
85 / dfw-north: 100 / dfw-outer: 130` ladder sits inside that band with a sensible
distance gradient. Nothing in the new 26-seller dataset is real, independent DFW
delivery-fee data (it is material pricing, with delivery fee models described but rarely
priced in dollars-per-mile detail beyond Texas Pure Products' and EarthMove's rows) —
so there is no new evidence to move these numbers on, and the 10-unit table shows they
already don't need to move to clear the broker-delivered bar.

confirmed.**

## Caveats

- **`concrete-sand` does not clear "below broker" even at `premiumRate = 0.25`** — its
  broker-delivered sample is thin (2 sellers: Gravelshop $61.92/ton, Hello Gravel
  $76.63/ton) and sits close to its own yard median ($59.26/ton), a much narrower
  yard-broker gap than every other sand SKU. This can't be fixed by lowering
  `premiumRate` alone without also compressing margin on the 21 SKUs that already clear
  the bar. Flag for a manual pricing decision (accept a below-cost-basis-relative-to-
  broker price on this one lower-volume SKU, wait for more broker samples, or price it
  as a "material-only, ask about delivery" outlier) before launch.
- **`brown-dyed` has zero broker-delivered comparison data** (0 broker sellers found) —
  its MGG-proposed price ($650/10yd) cannot be checked against the "below broker"
  requirement at all this pass.
- **Several SKUs clear broker by under 5%**: #57 limestone (98%), decomposed granite
  (99%), compost (95%), bank-sand (95%), cedar (98%). These are real numbers, not
  rounding — a single broker undercutting its own class median, or a slightly-stale
  yard price, could flip any of these. Re-check before launch, especially #57 and DG
  given they're also two of the highest-volume gravel SKUs.
- **City-page/SKU-count weighting** (unchanged from the first pass): sellers scraped
  across many product/city pages (Mulch Mound, Soil Building Systems 47 rows, Noles
  Enterprises 29 rows) carry proportionally more weight in medians than single-listing
  sellers. Not corrected here — no principled way to choose "weight by market-page
  coverage" vs. "weight sellers equally" with only 1-10 sellers per material. The
  clearest case: `garden-mix`'s yard pool is dominated by 38 rows, most from Soil
  Building Systems' specialty blend catalog (Gumbo Buster, Clay Slayer, Rose Mix,
  Potting Soil, etc.) — a genuine raised-bed/garden mix product mixed with premium
  amendment blends that are arguably a different product tier. Not filtered out (that
  would be a subjective curation call this pass declines to make silently); flagged
  here instead.
- **`river-rock`'s residual $49-$375/ton yard spread is legitimate, not a mapping bug**
  (the decorative-rock over-pooling bug is fixed — see "Corrections") — it reflects
  real size-grade variance (1" native river rock vs. 3-5" Colorado/Brazos blends cost
  meaningfully different amounts per ton). Same for the broker-delivered side
  ($61-$347/ton driven by the same size grading). Treat `river-rock`'s $87.13/ton node
  price as a blended average across grades, not a single SKU's true cost.
- **`decomposed-granite`'s yard spread is also wide** ($66-$154/ton, 8 sellers) —
  includes at least one colored/"Black" DG variant (Outdoor Warehouse Supply,
  $153.57/ton) pooled with natural DG. Kept pooled (same base material family, unlike
  river rock vs. decorative boulder) but the spread means the median is a rougher
  estimate than the 8-seller count alone suggests.
- **`bank-sand` has zero yard-class rows in the new 438-row CSV** — its node price is
  still the v1 scrape's single Mulch Mound observation ($51.11/yd retail → $43.44 at
  0.85). Lowest-confidence number in the catalog; would benefit most from a second data
  point of any slug here.
- **Luna Trucking's rows are numerically unusable.** It prices "per truckload" with no
  published load size, so `price_per_yd`/`price_per_ton` are blank for all 10 of its
  rows — kept in `prices-final.csv` (broker-class, delivered, local-Fort-Worth-only) as
  qualitative context, contributes zero rows to any median.
- **Node price = yard retail × 0.85, not a verified wholesale rate.** The 0.85 factor is
  a stated assumption (see Method), not observed data — it could be meaningfully wrong
  in either direction once a real partner contract exists.
- **Never invent prices**: every number in this document traces to a row in
  `prices-final.csv` (itself traceable to `products.csv`, a `verified: y` row in
  `external-llm-prices.csv`, or a row in the owner's `DFW_bulk-materials-pricing.csv`
  with its own `source_url` + `date_checked`), a figure supplied in the task brief (MGG
  current live prices), the stated 0.85 wholesale-factor assumption, or a documented
  carry-over placeholder (`bank-sand`) called out as such.
