# DFW Partner Cold Call Script

Status: template, 2026-09-28. For the owner (Eastern Landscape & Mason Supply / MGG) to
use when calling a DFW yard owner or manager. Yard-people-to-yard-people, not a
scripted-sounding cold-call — treat every bracketed line as a prompt to speak naturally
from, not a script to read verbatim. Recommended call order: Silver Creek Materials,
then Lowery Sand & Gravel, then Soil Building Systems (see `README.md` and
`partner-due-diligence.md` §6 for why).

---

## Opener (15 seconds)

> "Hey, is this [owner/manager name if known, else 'the person who handles wholesale/
> delivery accounts']? This is [name] — I run Eastern Landscape & Mason Supply, we're a
> yard out on Long Island, and I'm calling about a fulfillment partnership for a new
> online brand we're launching in DFW called MyGravelGuy. Got two minutes?"

If gatekeeper/front desk: "I'm looking for whoever handles new wholesale delivery
accounts — is that the owner, or do you have a dispatch manager I should talk to?"

---

## Who we are (ELM credibility — we're yard people too)

> "We're not a broker or a lead-gen site — I actually run a materials yard myself,
> Eastern Landscape & Mason Supply out in Center Moriches, New York. We've done close to
> 3,800 deliveries and about $1.4 million in delivered revenue over the last three
> years, so I know what it's like on your end of this call — I get calls from brokers
> all the time too and most of them are a headache. This is different: we're not asking
> you to do anything you don't already do."

---

## The ask

> "We built an online ordering site — MyGravelGuy — that sells gravel, mulch, sand, and
> soil delivered, and we're launching it in Dallas-Fort Worth. We don't have our own
> yard or trucks in DFW. What we're looking for is a local yard to actually fulfill the
> orders — you keep your normal pricing, we pay you promptly on our own dime, and you
> get orders with zero marketing cost and zero customer-service burden on your end,
> because we handle the customer, the payment, and the questions. You just load the
> truck and deliver like you already do every day."

Key framing, use as needed:
- "You're not giving anything up — same price, same trucks, same drivers."
- "We're bringing you orders you weren't going to get otherwise, not replacing anything
  you already have."
- "Think of us like one more phone that rings with a delivery order on it — except we
  already have the customer's card on file and we've confirmed the address."

## Qualification questions

Work through these naturally, don't read them as a checklist — this list maps directly
to `price-sheet-template.csv`'s columns, so the answers can go straight onto that sheet
after the call.

1. "What do you actually stock — gravel, mulch, sand, soil, all four?"
2. "What's your delivery radius, and do you split it into zones or price by mileage?"
   (map their answer against MGG's `dfw-core`/`dfw-north`/`dfw-outer` zones)
3. "What trucks do you run, and what's the max load on each?"
4. "Is there a minimum order for delivery? Any small-load surcharge?"
5. "What's your normal lead time — same day if ordered by when? Standard turnaround?"
6. "Do you deliver Saturdays? Is there a surcharge for that?"
7. "Do you have a cutoff time each day for next-day or same-day orders?"
8. "Do you already have contractor/trade pricing, and is that different from your
   posted/counter price?"
9. "How do you usually get paid on a wholesale/commercial account — net terms, COD,
   ACH? What's worked for you with other accounts like this?"
10. "Are you insured — general liability, auto liability on the delivery trucks? Could
    you send over a COI if we move forward?"
11. "How do you handle it today if a load gets placed wrong, or something's damaged at
    the drop — a driveway, a fence, whatever? Just want to understand your normal
    process."
12. "How do your drivers get their dispatch info day-to-day — phone, text, email, or do
    you run software for that?"

## Objection handling

**"We don't work with brokers."**
> "Totally fair, most broker relationships are bad for yards — they squeeze your price
> and disappear when something goes wrong. We're not doing that. You set your price, we
> don't touch it, and I'm a yard owner myself so I built this the way I'd want a
> partner to treat me."

**"You're going to undercut us / sell our own material back cheaper than we do."**
> "The opposite, actually — we're priced *above* what you'd charge walk-in, because our
> customers are paying for a guaranteed online experience, not the lowest price. You
> keep your normal price to us; we mark it up on our end, not the other way around."

**"Who owns the customer?"**
> "We do — MyGravelGuy takes the order, the payment, and the relationship. You're the
> fulfillment side, same as if you had a second sales channel you didn't have to run
> yourself. If it doesn't work out for either of us, you're not out anything — you
> weren't getting those orders before."

**"We're too busy in spring to take on anything new."**
> "That's actually the best reason to try it in the off-season first — we can start
> slow, a handful of loads a week, and you tell us when you want us to dial it up or
> down. We're not asking for a capacity commitment, just a price sheet and a way to
> reach your dispatcher."

**"What's actually in it for us?"**
> "Orders you weren't going to get otherwise, paid promptly, with zero ad spend and zero
> customer service on your side — we're doing the website, the marketing, the payment
> processing, and the customer texts. You're doing what you already do best: loading
> trucks and driving."

**"How do I know you'll actually pay?"**
> "Fair question — happy to do net-7 or weekly ACH after delivery confirmation, whatever
> feels safest to start. We can start with a smaller volume until there's trust built up
> on both sides. I run a real yard myself, this isn't a fly-by-night operation."

**"What if there's a damage claim — a driveway, a fence, whatever?"**
> "We'll put that in writing up front — normal industry practice, a placement waiver the
> customer signs before we dispatch, and a clear process for who handles what if
> something does go wrong. I've dealt with this on my own yard's trucks, I'm not going
> to leave you exposed."

**"Why wouldn't we just list ourselves on Hello Gravel or one of those sites instead?"**
> "You could, and some yards do both — no reason not to. The difference is those are
> national platforms that don't know DFW and don't know you; we're two yard operators
> building something together, and you're talking to the actual owner, not a support
> ticket. Try both, see what actually sends you orders."

**"We already have more delivery business than we can handle."**
> "Then this is easy — tell me what capacity you *do* have, even if it's small, and
> we'll only send what fits. No obligation to take more than that."
